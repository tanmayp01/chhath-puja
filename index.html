What is Chhath?

Chhath is a four-day festival of thanks to Surya Dev, the Sun, and to Chhathi Maiya, who is prayed to for the health and long life of children and family. It falls on Kartik Shukla Shashthi, six days after Diwali.

It is the great festival of Bihar, Jharkhand, eastern Uttar Pradesh and Nepal's Madhesh, and today it lights up riverbanks, ponds and even rooftop tanks from Delhi and Mumbai to Mauritius, Dubai and New Jersey.

Chhath is rare in honouring the setting sun as well as the rising one. There is no priest and no idol: the vratin (the person keeping the fast) stands in water and offers arghya directly to the Sun.

The four days in 2026

13
Nov
Day 1 · Friday

Nahay Khay

A holy bath and a spotless home. The vratin eats one pure meal, traditionally kaddu-bhaat: bottle gourd, chana dal and rice cooked with ghee and rock salt.

14
Nov
Day 2 · Saturday

Kharna

A day-long fast, broken at dusk with rasiya (jaggery kheer) and roti cooked on a clay chulha with mango wood. After this prasad, the 36-hour nirjala fast begins, with not even water.

15
Nov
Day 3 · Sunday

Sandhya Arghya

Thekua is fried at home, the daura is packed with fruit, and the family walks to the ghat singing. At sunset the vratin stands in the water and offers arghya to the setting sun. At night comes kosi bharai: diyas lit beneath a canopy of sugarcane.

16
Nov
Day 4 · Monday

Usha Arghya & Paran

Back at the ghat before first light. Arghya is offered to the rising sun, prasad is shared with everyone, and the vratin breaks the fast (paran).

What goes in the soop

  • Thekua: wheat flour, jaggery and ghee, pressed with a wooden mould and fried
  • Kasar or ladua: rice-flour and jaggery laddoos
  • Whole sugarcane, a bunch of bananas and a coconut
  • Big gagal nimbu (citron), sweet lime and singhara (water chestnut)
  • Fresh ginger and turmeric plants, radish and seasonal produce
  • A diya, sindoor and a lota of water or milk for the arghya

Sounds of the ghat

Chhath geet are sung all four days, mostly in Bhojpuri, Maithili and Magahi. The voice of the late Sharda Sinha is so tied to the festival that her songs are the soundtrack of most ghats.

Ghat etiquette

  • Give vratins space at the water's edge; they have not had water for a day and a half.
  • Keep the river clean. Carry back plastic and leave only flowers and diyas.
  • Never step over a soop, daura or prasad.
  • Watch children near the water and stay inside the barricades.
  • Accept prasad with both hands, and offer it to whoever is near you.
